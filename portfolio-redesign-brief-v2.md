## 2. Design concept

### Working concept: Personal archive / creative developer studio

Think of the website as a personal archive of things Benjamin builds, makes, listens to, and experiments with.

It should sit somewhere between:

- A software developer portfolio.
- A music / creative person's personal site.
- An experimental graphic-design publication.
- A small digital archive.

Avoid making it look like a generic SaaS landing page, a standard dark-mode dashboard, or a music streaming service.

### Personality

- Personal, observant, creative, and a little playful.
- Visually expressive without feeling random.
- Professional without sounding corporate.
- A balance of polished engineering and unfinished creative curiosity.

### Design principles

1. **Atmosphere first, clarity always.** Strong imagery and layout can create mood, but content must remain readable.
2. **Use contrast intentionally.** Pair dark, warm photography with quiet neutral space and occasional high-energy graphic moments.
3. **Make each project feel distinct.** Do not force every project into the same card template.
4. **Let personal work coexist with engineering.** Music and visual experiments are part of the identity, not unrelated decorations.
5. **Do not over-design the interface.** The graphics should frame the work, not obscure it.

## 3. Visual system

### Colour palette

Starting palette to test, not a rigid final specification:

| Role | Suggested colour | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Main background | `#171512` | Primary dark canvas |
| Raised surface | `#24201B` | Panels / secondary areas |
| Warm brown | `#493322` | Image framing / subtle surfaces |
| Burnt amber | `#A85F2C` | Accent details |
| Warm amber | `#D69A4B` | Links, highlights, small labels |
| Warm paper | `#E9DDC9` | Main text / occasional light surface |
| Muted text | `#A79A88` | Secondary copy |

Use amber as a warm accent and let photography provide much of the orange/yellow light. Do not make every button, heading, border, and icon amber.

**Optional accent colours:** occasional saturated yellow, red, green, or electric blue may appear in project-specific artwork or experimental graphic moments, inspired by the reference board. These should be used sparingly and should not undermine the site's overall cohesion.

### Typography

Use a restrained type system with room for expressive display moments:

- **Primary sans-serif:** Instrument Sans or a similar contemporary grotesk for body text, navigation, and interface.
- **Display type:** a bold, condensed, or expressive face for occasional large headings and poster-like project titles.
- **Optional serif:** use sparingly for editorial contrast if it genuinely improves the design.
- **Metadata:** small uppercase labels, project numbers, dates, roles, and technical tags.

Do not use too many typefaces. Aim for two families, with a third only if there is a clear reason.

### Texture and image treatment

Possible treatments:

- Fine film grain or photocopy texture in selected areas.
- Warm highlights and deep shadows in photography.
- Slightly imperfect crops and layered image edges.
- Thin rules, small labels, and editorial captions.
- Occasional halftone, scanline, or photocopied effects as decorative accents.

Do not apply heavy grain, blur, scanlines, or color filters to every image. Preserve image clarity and accessibility. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Layout and composition

- Use a flexible grid rather than repeating identical cards.
- Combine full-width feature areas with smaller supporting items.
- Use asymmetry and overlap selectively.
- Let project imagery have different aspect ratios where it suits the content.
- Use generous spacing around important text.
- Use small editorial details such as `01 / SELECTED WORK`, `CALGARY, AB`, or project-category labels.
- Ensure the layout still collapses cleanly on mobile.

## 8. Responsive design and accessibility

- Mobile-first implementation.
- Test phone, tablet, laptop, and wide-screen layouts.
- Semantic landmarks and logical heading order.
- Keyboard navigation and visible focus states.
- Sufficient contrast, especially over photography.
- Meaningful alt text for informative images.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- No horizontal overflow.
- Accessible media controls and no autoplay.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Optimize image sizes and prevent layout shifts.

Experimental design must not come at the expense of usability.

---

## 9. Technical implementation

First inspect the existing repository, framework, routes, components, styling conventions, and deployment setup.

- Preserve the existing stack and working infrastructure unless there is a clear reason to change it.
- Reuse existing dependencies where practical.
- Do not introduce a CMS, database, authentication, backend, or paid service just for a static portfolio.
- Keep project content in maintainable data structures.
- Add or update page metadata, Open Graph image, favicon, and page descriptions.
- Check every internal and external link.
- Do not add analytics or tracking without asking.
- Remove all temporary placeholders before deployment.
